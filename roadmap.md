# agent-sessions roadmap

Current package version: **0.2.0**. The changes below are unreleased.

## Current priority

No paid product wedge is currently supported by the evidence. Search, recall, handoff, recovery, replay, privacy scanning, verification, active-path retrieval, and context debugging have direct competitors or no demonstrated user advantage. The encoded-secret-to-committed-line hypothesis remains technically unproven and is closed as a product lead: its synthetic prototype works, but separate Skarn and DeepBlame runs did not compare the combined workflow, Gitleaks was unavailable, and reviewer effort was not measured. The Pi context-debugging test showed that Pi's existing provider trace plus its saved session and changed file could explain one synthetic context-loss case. AgentPlay and multi-agent OpenTelemetry dashboards cover the wider replay/telemetry workflow. Close context capture as a product feature; do not add another trace viewer. October 28 issue screening found reports of repeated rule violations, but existing runtime guardrails cover the broad response. The approval-to-effect integrity lead is closed as a generic product direction after a synthetic Claude tool-layer probe and review of existing effect-observation projects; the probe showed a mismatch but established no distinct value. A separate approval-provenance screen found GitHub review controls and a direct evidence-product overlap; the one-organization case report does not establish a distinct wedge. A synthetic two-worktree integration probe reproduced a cleanly merged, cross-file semantic failure; this repository preserved the session statements and edits but did not diagnose the mismatch. Foremerge covers proactive coordination; TrajAudit, AgentRx, AgentDebugX, agenttrace, and onpath cover adjacent diagnosis or local incident reporting. Close generic multi-agent diagnosis. A new screen found recent provider-history loss reports, but conformance, preservation, archive, conversion, and recovery tools already cover much of that area. **Migration fidelity checking is no longer the leading product candidate:** the Codex CLI 0.164.0 synthetic reproduction remains useful parser/correctness evidence, but Moirai and session-migrate document cross-agent conversion, loss reporting, and validation, so a broad migration checker lacks a supported differentiation claim. The October 28 test-freshness/agent-claim lead is also closed: Backcheck, Yoetz, trustbut, evigate, and Treft cover transcript freshness, cross-agent receipts, independent test evidence, or clean PR checks. **Generic requirement continuity across compaction is closed:** the open-source `compaction-loss-check` skill inspects Claude compaction summaries and standing rules, while Compaction.dev markets per-turn comparison and preservation across Claude Code, Codex, and Cursor. Reopen only for a specific costly failure both miss. Cross-agent retention-policy reconciliation has a contract-level synthetic comparison with no incremental mismatch over an equally current manual register. Keep only the unanswered question of drift frequency and review savings; no product prototype is justified yet. Agent-memory deletion verification is now directly overlapped by memory lifecycle benchmarks and a provable-deletion product. Session-to-workspace binding was screened and withdrawn as a product lead: Ordo and HOL Guard cover cross-agent policies, while sandbox probes and native controls test or constrain boundaries. Broad cross-agent continuity is now also screened out: Motif and Crosshead publicly document overlapping session memory, source-backed handoffs, and continuity workflows. No candidate has established uniqueness, prevalence, adoption, or willingness to pay.
Private history stays local. Repository exploration and public reports do not establish prevalence, product distinction, adoption, or willingness to pay.
